COMPARISON BUILDER · COMPANION VOLUME

The Comparison Builder Companion

A complete guide to the 4% Rule vs Sovereign Glidepath historical comparison tool

Matches Comparison Builder's field names and toggle labels throughout

Refers to Comparison Builder Build 1.0.2

About This Book

Comparison Builder is a standalone companion to Sovereign Glidepath. Where the main app manages one real plan going forward, Comparison Builder asks a narrower, sharper question about the past: if you'd retired in 1970, or 1971, or any other year the historical data allows, using either the classic 4% Rule or the real Sovereign Glidepath engine, what would actually have happened to your money?

It runs both strategies against every rolling historical start year the data supports, side by side, using the same Guyton-Klinger engine and defensive bucket logic as the main app, and reports what was actually spent — not just what happened to be left over at the end.

The Complete Beginner

New to retirement math — wants to see, in plain terms, why a fixed 4% rule and a flexible guardrail rule can behave so differently over the same real history.

The Experienced DIYer

Already running Sovereign Glidepath for real, wants concrete historical evidence for how much more — or less — could safely have been drawn.

The IFA / Professional

Wants the exact ranking rules, the workbook's methodology, and the formulas behind every figure on screen.

Every chapter is built the same way, in three layers:

  1. EBOOK SECTION — the idea, in plain language, with an analogy.
  2. MANUAL SECTION — the exact UI reference: every field, toggle, and button, and how to reproduce a specific test.
  3. TEACHING AID SECTION — how to use this in your own analysis, a checklist, review questions, and a hands-on challenge.

A field name in this book and a field name on your screen are always the same string — e.g. Classic 4% Rule Withdrawal (£), State Pension Starts At Age.

Part I — Testing a Plan Against Real History

1. Rolling Historical Cohorts & the Honest Scorecard

1.1 Ebook Section — The "Why"

Comparison Builder doesn't ask "what's the average return going to be?" It asks a much blunter question: if you'd retired in 1970, lived through the real, actual sequence of market returns and inflation that followed, and drawn a fixed strategy the whole way through, what would have happened? Then it closes 1970's retirement and opens 1971's, and repeats — for every start year the underlying data can support a full retirement horizon for. Each one of those start years is a cohort: one complete, real, lived retirement.

This is a deliberately different exercise from Pane 5's Monte Carlo simulator. Pane 5 asks "what might the future look like, across 10,000 imagined futures?" Comparison Builder asks "what did the past actually do, across every real 30-year stretch we have data for?" Both are useful; neither substitutes for the other.

ANALOGY

Think of the historical data as a long, unbroken filmstrip of market history. Comparison Builder places a fixed-length window over the start of that filmstrip, plays the whole scene through to the end, records exactly what happened, then slides the window forward by one year and does it again — until the window would run off the end of the available film.

This also settles a famous claim on its own terms. "The 4% Rule never failed historically" is true only under specific conditions — Comparison Builder lets you test whether it holds for your numbers, your market choice, and your inflation-rule setting, rather than taking the claim on faith.

1.2 Manual Section — Fields & Effects

Control Effect
Global Equities Pot (£) / Cash Pot (£) Your starting position. The two are added together as the combined starting pot for both strategies — Classic 4% Rule Withdrawal auto-fills to 4% of this total until you edit it directly.
Starting Age The age retirement begins. Feeds Sovereign Glidepath's Go-Go / Go-Slow / No-Go phase logic (see Chapter 2) and anchors the State Pension Starts At Age comparison (Chapter 3).
Horizon (years) The length of every simulated retirement. Also determines how many rolling cohorts exist — a longer horizon means fewer possible start years, since each one needs that many full years of data ahead of it.
Classic 4% Rule Withdrawal (£) Auto-fills to 4% of the combined starting pot, and re-syncs whenever the pot fields change — until you edit it directly, at which point it stops auto-following (a note under the field confirms this).
SG Target Annual Withdrawal (£) Sovereign Glidepath's own withdrawal target — entirely independent of the Classic field. A live note shows it as a percentage of the Classic baseline, so you always know how the two starting points relate.
Run Comparison button Runs every rolling cohort the current Market/Horizon settings support. The tool also runs automatically once on page load, using the pre-filled example values.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT — IFA / advanced readers

Underlying data: S&P 500, 10-Year US Treasury Bonds, 3-month US T-Bills, and US CPI, 1928–2025 (Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern), plus MSCI World gross annual returns, 1970–2025. Choosing "Global" on either the Classic or SG side switches that column's equity leg to MSCI World. As of Build 1.0.2, selecting Global on either side also switches both columns' inflation and cash/money-market legs to UK-sourced data — ONS RPI (series CZBH) for inflation, and a Bank of England Bank Rate / OECD UK 3-month interbank splice for cash — since these no longer make sense pinned to the US when the plan itself is meant to be UK-focused. Bonds remain US Treasury-sourced in both modes: no free equivalent to the Damodaran dataset exists for UK gilts (the DMS/Credit Suisse Global Investment Returns Yearbook, which does have this data, sits behind a paid licence), so this is a stated limitation rather than an approximation. Because MSCI World only starts in 1970, selecting Global anywhere still restricts every cohort to start no earlier than 1970, regardless of the other side's setting.

Try it yourself: Section 1 comes pre-filled with example values so the tool has something to show you immediately — replace them with your own numbers to match your real plan (a future version may pull these in automatically from your real Pane 1 plan instead). Whatever figures end up in Section 1, the exact cohort count and start-year range Comparison Builder will test always appears in the note under the Run Comparison button before you even click it — as a general rule, a longer Horizon or selecting Global on either side will always narrow that range, for the reasons explained above.

1.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • A "cohort" is one complete, real, lived retirement starting in one specific historical year.
  • Comparison Builder tests every rolling cohort the data allows, not just one hand-picked example.
  • Choosing "Global" anywhere restricts every cohort to 1970 onward, since MSCI World starts there.
  • Global also switches inflation and cash to UK-sourced series for both columns — only bonds stay US-sourced, since no free UK gilt data exists.
  • Famous rules-of-thumb are only as true as the specific data and settings they're tested against.

Review Questions:

  1. (Conceptual) Why does testing 27 rolling cohorts tell you more than testing a single hand-picked 30-year period?
  2. (Practical) You set Horizon to 40 years but leave both Market toggles on "US Only". What is the earliest possible cohort start year, and why?
  3. (Practical) You then switch the Classic Market toggle to "Global (MSCI World)", leaving SG on "US Only". Does the earliest possible start year change? Why?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Set Horizon to 20 years with both Market toggles on "US Only", and note the cohort range in the note under Run Comparison. Now raise Horizon to 40 years. Confirm the range of possible start years shrinks from both ends being pinned by the same 1928–2025 data window, and explain in your own words why a longer horizon always means fewer cohorts.

Part II — Two Engines, Side by Side

2. Classic 4% Rule vs Sovereign Glidepath

2.1 Ebook Section

The left-hand column is a faithful replica of William Bengen's original 4% Rule: a fixed stock/bond split, a withdrawal that escalates with inflation every year, and no response whatsoever to how markets are actually performing. The right-hand column is the real Sovereign Glidepath engine — the same Guyton-Klinger guardrail logic and two-bucket defensive draw used by the main app — ported line-by-line and verified against it.

ANALOGY

Classic is a metronome: the same fixed beat, indifferent to whatever the orchestra is doing around it. Sovereign Glidepath is a driver reading the road: easing off when conditions are bad, allowed to speed up a little when conditions are clearly good, and driving flat-out with no restrictions at all once the phase of the journey no longer calls for caution.

One important consequence: SG's cash buffer is sized in months of withdrawal, not a fixed percentage split — realistic to how the real app behaves, but it means SG typically ends up far more equity-heavy (roughly 85–90%) than Classic's chosen 60/40 or 50/50 split, purely as an arithmetic consequence of how the buffer is sized.

2.2 Manual Section — Fields & Effects

Control Effect
Portfolio Split — 60/40 or 50/50 (Classic only) Fixed equities/bonds weighting, held constant for the entire cohort — never rebalanced by performance.
Market — US Only / Global (both columns, set independently) Swaps that column's equity leg between S&P 500 and MSCI World. Selecting Global on either column also switches inflation and cash for both columns to UK-sourced series (ONS RPI; a BoE Bank Rate / OECD interbank splice) — bonds stay 10-Year US Treasury regardless of this choice, since no free UK gilt data exists.
Inflation Rule — Always rises with inflation (default) The standard, unmodified Bengen rule: withdrawal escalates with CPI every single year, regardless of how markets performed.
Inflation Rule — Skip rise after a down year A small, one-condition simplification of Bengen's own original "Inflation Rule": if the portfolio had a negative real return last year, this year's withdrawal stays the same £ amount (no CPI rise) — but a genuine deflationary cut is never skipped, since skipping a cut would perversely increase real spending right after a bad year.
Cash Buffer — 12 / 24 / 36 months (SG only) Sized in months of SG's own withdrawal target, not a fixed percentage — recalculated whenever the withdrawal figure changes.
Defensive Draw Threshold — Strict / Standard / Aggressive (SG only) How readily SG switches to cash-first spending for a given period. See the technical callout below for the exact hurdles.
Tick Mode — Yearly / Quarterly (SG only) Quarterly re-checks the Guyton-Klinger guardrail four times a year instead of once, matching how the live app behaves when you commit a ledger row each quarter — usually a slightly firmer worst-case floor.
Guardrail Depth — Flat -10% / Graduated -10% to -20% (SG only) Flat: every Preservation cut is exactly -10%, however long the bad stretch lasts. Graduated: cuts deepen the longer Preservation stays active consecutively (-10% → -15% → -20%, capped).

TECHNICAL CALLOUT — IFA / advanced readers

SG's guardrail: if the current withdrawal rate has risen to ≥120% of its all-time-high-anchored reference, apply a Preservation cut (-10% flat, or graduated -10%/-15%/-20% by consecutive Preservation periods). If it has fallen to ≤80% of the plan's baseline rate, apply a Prosperity rise (+10%). The guardrail switches off entirely from age 86 onward (the No-Go phase), and withdrawals proceed at the baseline rate with no further adjustment. The Defensive Draw Threshold hurdle is phase-dependent: Standard uses 10% drawdown from peak (15% in Go-Slow, ages 76–85); Strict uses 20% (25% in Go-Slow); Aggressive uses half the Standard hurdle. Below the hurdle, that period draws from Cash first (subject to Cash actually covering it); otherwise spending comes from Equities, with any surplus swept into Cash up to its target buffer size.

Reproducible test: with identical inputs, switching the Guardrail Depth from Flat to Graduated should never make a surviving cohort worse — it can only deepen a cut that Flat would have applied anyway during an unusually long run of bad years, trading a firmer near-term cut for (typically) a smaller number of consecutive cut periods.

2.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • Classic is fixed and unresponsive to markets; SG dynamically cuts or raises withdrawals by guardrail.
  • Classic's bond leg is always 10-Year US Treasuries, whatever the Market toggle says — the one leg Global never touches.
  • The "skip rise" toggle only ever skips a positive CPI rise, never a deflationary cut.
  • SG's cash buffer is sized in months of withdrawal, which naturally makes it far more equity-heavy than Classic.
  • SG's guardrail switches off completely from age 86 onward (No-Go phase).

Review Questions:

  1. (Conceptual) Why does skipping a deflationary CPI cut (rather than only a rise) make the "skip after a down year" rule less protective, not more?
  2. (Practical) You set Classic to "Global (MSCI World)" and leave its Portfolio Split at 60/40. Which part of Classic's return, if any, is still US-sourced — and which parts switch to UK data?
  3. (Practical) At what age does SG's guardrail stop adjusting withdrawals altogether?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Run the default scenario, note SG's Avg Final Value in the Overall Comparison panel. Switch Cash Buffer from 24 to 36 months and re-run. Explain the direction of the change using what you now know about how the buffer size is calculated and what it displaces.

Part III — Modelling a State Pension

3. Pensions and the Reduced Draw

3.1 Ebook Section

A state pension changes the arithmetic of a retirement plan, because it arrives every year regardless of markets. Comparison Builder lets you add one optional, flat, real-terms pension to both strategies at once. Leave the start age blank and neither strategy sees any pension at all — the default, and the honest baseline for comparing the two rules on the pot alone.

Because the figure you enter rises with inflation every year once it starts, it holds one constant real (today's-money) value for every year it's paid — there's no separate escalation to configure. What changes is simple: from the year the pension starts, both models draw less from the pot, pound for pound, to deliver the exact same target spending the retiree was always going to receive.

3.2 Manual Section — Fields & Effects

Control Effect
State Pension Starts At Age Left blank by default — no pension applies to either model. Once set, both models see an identical pension from that age onward.
State Pension Amount (£/yr, today's money) The flat real amount paid every year from the start age onward. Reduces the amount drawn from the pot(s), never the target the retiree receives (until the pot itself can no longer cover the gap).
Live coverage note Shows the pension as a percentage of both the Classic and SG withdrawal targets, updating as any of the four related fields change.

NO AGE, NO PENSION

Filling in an amount but leaving the start age blank has no effect whatsoever — both models still draw their full target from the pot every year. If a run doesn't show the pension impact you expect, check the age field first.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT

Each model still targets the same gross withdrawal the underlying rule calls for in that year (Classic's CPI-escalated figure, or SG's guardrail-adjusted figure); the pension is netted off after that target is set, and only the shortfall is actually drawn from the pot. If the pension figure equals or exceeds the target, the pot draw for that period is floored at zero rather than going negative. Because the pension is independent of the portfolio, it keeps paying even in a cohort where the pot itself runs dry.

Try it yourself: whatever numbers are loaded into Section 1, note SG's Could Have Safely Spent figure (see Chapter 5's Spare Capacity) for any one cohort with no pension configured. Add a sensible pension start age and amount and re-run. That figure should never fall — it can only hold steady or rise, since a pension can only ever reduce the burden on the pot, never add to it. If it ever fell after adding a pension, that would indicate something had gone wrong.

3.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • No start age means no pension at all — this is the default and the honest baseline.
  • The pension figure is real-terms and flat once claimed — it rises with inflation by definition.
  • Both models receive an identical pension; only the amount drawn from each pot differs.
  • The pension keeps paying even in cohorts where the pot itself is exhausted.

Review Questions:

  1. (Conceptual) Why does a state pension that "rises with inflation" need no separate escalation setting in this real-terms model?
  2. (Practical) You enter £15,000 into State Pension Amount but leave the start age blank. What happens when you run the comparison?
  3. (Practical) A cohort's SG pot is fully exhausted in year 22 of a 30-year horizon, and the pension started in year 5. Does the pension continue to be paid in years 23–30?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Run the default scenario with no pension, and note the SG Spare Capacity figure for the earliest cohort. Add a realistic pension (start age, and a sensible annual amount) and re-run. Confirm the spare capacity rises, and explain why using what you now know about how the pension offsets the pot draw.

Part IV — Reading the Chart

4. The Bar Chart, the Spend Trajectory & Tooltips

4.1 Ebook Section

The main chart's bars show total real lifetime spending delivered for each cohort — not the final pot value. This is a deliberate choice: final value alone rewards under-spending (or dying with money left on the table), which isn't a better retirement on its own. A dashed red border on a bar means that strategy ran out of money in that cohort — subtle by design, so a handful of failures don't visually overwhelm the whole chart.

Click any bar to select that cohort. The Spend Trajectory chart below redraws to show that specific retirement's year-by-year real withdrawal for both strategies, and hovering it reveals a tooltip with the exact figures for that single year.

ANALOGY

The bar chart is the summary on the back of the book; the spend trajectory is opening it to the actual page for one specific year of one specific retirement.

4.2 Manual Section — Fields & Effects

Element Meaning
Bar chart (amber = Classic, green = SG) Total real £ actually spent over the full retirement, by cohort start year — not final pot value.
Dashed red border on a bar That strategy ran out of money in that cohort. Click the bar to see exactly which year.
Fail tooltip (on the selected bar only) A small horizontal pop-up naming the exact ruin year for whichever strategy (or both) failed in the currently-selected cohort — shown only for the selected bar, to avoid label pile-up across every cohort.
Spend Trajectory chart Year-by-year real withdrawal for both strategies, for whichever cohort is currently selected (defaults to the earliest available on load).
Spend Trajectory tooltip (on hover) Shows the retirement year, age, and calendar year; state pension in payment (if any) for that year; each strategy's total spend and, if a pension is active, how much of that came from the pot; and the cumulative SG-vs-Classic difference to that point.
Trajectory stat grid Per-cohort figures: Avg Annual Spend (both), extra/less drawn on average, total extra spending provided or given up, Peak Drawdown (both), Spare Capacity (or "n/a — ran out"), and State Pension Received over the full horizon if configured.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT

Earlier builds drew the ruin label as small rotated (vertical) text directly on the canvas next to the failed bar — compact, but hard to read. It was replaced with a normal horizontal tooltip, positioned above the bar using the canvas's on-page location so it stays anchored correctly even if the page is scrolled. If both strategies fail in the same selected cohort, both ruin years are shown together in one tooltip.

Reproducible test: click the leftmost (earliest) bar, then the rightmost (latest) bar, and confirm the Spend Trajectory chart and its hint text below the chart update to match each selection, and that the selected bar gets a highlighted border on the main chart.

4.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • Bar height is total lifetime spend delivered, deliberately not final pot value.
  • A dashed red border flags a ruin; the exact year only appears once you select that bar.
  • The Spend Trajectory chart always reflects whichever bar is currently selected.
  • The hover tooltip is the only place pension and pot-draw figures appear side by side, year by year.

Review Questions:

  1. (Conceptual) Why might ranking cohorts by final pot value alone be misleading?
  2. (Practical) How do you find out exactly which year a given cohort's Classic strategy ran out?
  3. (Practical) With a pension configured, what two figures does the Spend Trajectory tooltip show for a year the pension is active, beyond the total spend?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Find a cohort with a dashed red border on either bar (raise the withdrawal fields if none appear by default). Select it, read the fail tooltip, then hover the Spend Trajectory chart near the end of that strategy's line to confirm the year matches.

Part V — The Overall Verdict

5. Safety, Spend, Hardship & Spare Capacity

5.1 Ebook Section

Rather than boil everything down to one misleading "which strategy won" score, the Overall Comparison panel deliberately splits the verdict into four honest categories: Safety (did it run out at all), Total Lifetime Spending Delivered (what was actually spent, averaged across every cohort), Hardship Years (how rough the worst years got), and Spare Capacity / Final Value (explicitly reframed as "the ability to have spent even more", not a scoreboard).

Spare Capacity is not an estimate — for every cohort, a genuine binary search re-runs SG's full simulation against that cohort's actual historical returns to find the maximum withdrawal it could have sustained without running out. It answers, in hindsight, "given how this specific stretch of history actually played out, how much more could I safely have spent?"

5.2 Manual Section — Fields, Statistics & Effects

Statistic Meaning
Classic / SG Ran Out How many of the tested cohorts ended in ruin for that strategy, out of the total tested.
Classic / SG Avg Lifetime Spend Average total real spending delivered across every tested cohort.
SG Delivered More Spending In Number of cohorts where SG's total lifetime spend beat Classic's.
Classic / SG Avg Worst Year The average, across cohorts, of each strategy's single worst annual spend.
Classic / SG Hardship Years (<80% of target) Percentage of all lived years, across every cohort, where that year's spend fell below 80% of the strategy's target.
Classic / SG Avg Final Value Average leftover pot value at the end of the horizon — spare capacity, not a measure of a better retirement on its own.
SG Could Have Safely Spent (Avg/yr) Average, across cohorts, of how much more SG's binary search found could have been drawn without ruin.
Cohorts With Room to Spend More How many cohorts showed any positive spare capacity at all.
State Pension Impact (only shown if a pension is configured) Pension start age, annual amount, and the total received over the full horizon — identical for every cohort, since it depends only on age and horizon, not on market history.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT — IFA / advanced readers

The Spare Capacity search re-sizes SG's cash buffer at each trial withdrawal to stay at the configured number of months of that trial withdrawal, matching how the buffer genuinely behaves in the live app (it is defined in months of current withdrawal, not a fixed £ figure). The search runs roughly 25 iterations of bisection per cohort, converging to within about £25/yr — around 40ms for all cohorts combined, a negligible cost against the main comparison.

Reproducible test: a cohort with any Ruin Year shows "n/a — ran out" for its own Spare Capacity in the per-cohort panel, since there is no room to spend more in a cohort that already failed at the actual withdrawal rate — the search only ever reports a positive figure for cohorts that survived.

5.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • There is no single combined "score" — Safety, Spend, Hardship, and Spare Capacity are kept separate on purpose.
  • Spare Capacity is a genuine re-simulation per cohort, not an estimate or a rule of thumb.
  • A cohort that already ran out cannot show positive spare capacity — it shows "n/a" instead.
  • The State Pension Impact figures are identical across all cohorts of the same age/horizon by construction.

Review Questions:

  1. (Conceptual) Why does the tool avoid reducing the comparison to one overall "winner" score?
  2. (Practical) Which single statistic should you check first to judge basic safety?
  3. (Practical) Why is the State Pension Impact category's "Total Received Over Full Horizon" figure the same for every cohort tested?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Note the SG Hardship Years percentage and SG Could Have Safely Spent figure for the default scenario. Raise SG's Target Annual Withdrawal by 20% and re-run. Confirm Hardship Years rises and Spare Capacity falls, and explain the relationship between the two in your own words.

Part VI — Taking It With You

6. The Excel Workbook

6.1 Ebook Section

The Download Excel Workbook button hands you the entire analysis to keep, print, or share — styled to look deliberately put-together rather than a bare data dump, on a printable white/light-grey background with the corporate logo and the amber/green colour-coding carrying the brand through.

Beyond the full Summary of every cohort, the workbook pulls out six specific cohorts for a closer look: the best, median, and worst historical cohort for each strategy, by lifetime spend delivered. If a year happens to hold the same rank for both strategies, or two ranks for one strategy, it only gets a single sheet — labelled with every tag that applies.

Each of those ledger sheets also lists that year's own inflation rate and raw market returns for both strategies, alongside the resulting pot values — so you can see exactly what market conditions produced a given year's movement, not just the movement itself. Alongside SG's real pot draw, a matching SG Nominal Spend (£) column shows the same figure converted into that year's own money, directly parallel to Classic's Nominal Spend column. And SG's internal bucket mechanics are now fully visible: a Drawn From Equities (£) and Drawn From Cash (£) pair shows exactly which bucket funded that period's spending, and a Cash Refill (£) column shows the amount (if any) moved from Equities back into Cash that period to top the buffer back up. Equities are never moved into Cash except as this refill — there's no reverse "Cash into Equities" transfer in the model.

6.2 Manual Section — Fields & Effects

Sheet Contents
Overview Cover page: logo, scenario parameters panel (pot, age/horizon, both strategies' settings, pension, cohort count), and a Headline Results table naming the best/median/worst cohort for each strategy.
Summary Every tested cohort, one row each: final values, ruin years, lifetime spend, worst year, hardship years, spare capacity, and Classic/SG Rank tags. Ruined cells get a dashed red border, echoing the chart's own fail styling.
Ledger sheets (one per distinct best/median/worst cohort) Full year-by-year breakdown for that single cohort: that year's inflation rate and raw market returns (Classic's equity and bond, SG's equity and cash — negative years picked out in red), state pension, Classic's nominal spend and real pot draw, Classic's pot value, SG's nominal spend, SG's guardrail status (colour-coded), SG's pot draw, SG's Drawn From Equities / Drawn From Cash split, SG's Cash Refill amount, and SG's Equities/Cash/Total.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT — IFA / advanced readers

Ranking rule: a ruin always ranks worse than any surviving cohort for that strategy. Among survivors, ranking is by total real lifetime spend delivered — with final pot value folded in as a small tiebreaker (scaled to well under £1 of influence). The tiebreaker matters specifically because, under "Always rises with inflation", Classic's real withdrawal is the exact same fixed amount every single year regardless of market returns, as long as the pot never runs dry — so every surviving Classic cohort delivers an identical total lifetime spend, and final value becomes the only thing that genuinely distinguishes best from median from worst.

TECHNICAL CALLOUT — the guardrail's No-Go label

The Guyton-Klinger guardrail is structurally switched off once a cohort reaches No-Go phase (age 86+ by default) — spending simply tracks target from then on, with no Preservation or Prosperity adjustment possible. The SG Guardrail column marks these years "Normal (No-Go)" rather than a plain "Normal", so a genuinely unremarkable in-range year and a year where the guardrail can no longer act are never visually indistinguishable. Depending on the Guardrail Depth toggle, you may also see "Preservation (-10%)", escalating to "(-15%)" and "(-20%)" under Graduated depth for consecutive Preservation years, or a flat "Preservation" (always -10%) under Flat depth — and "Prosperity" whenever spend has fallen well enough below baseline to earn a raise. Which of these actually appear in your own workbook depends entirely on whether that cohort's history ever pushed it into each zone — a calmer cohort may show only "Normal" and "Preservation" throughout.

Reproducible test: with the default scenario (Always rises with inflation, no ruins), every surviving Classic cohort's Lifetime Spend column reads exactly the same figure in the Summary sheet — confirm this, then check that the Classic Best/Median/Worst tags still land on different years, distinguished purely by final value.

6.3 Teaching Aid Section

Key takeaways:

  • The workbook is styled for print — light background, dark text, coloured accents and logo only.
  • Six candidate cohorts are pulled out for detailed ledgers: best/median/worst for each strategy.
  • A ruin always ranks worst; survivors are ranked by lifetime spend, tie-broken by final value.
  • Ledger sheets share full year-by-year detail, including state pension and pot-draw columns.
  • SG's Drawn From Equities / Drawn From Cash / Cash Refill columns make every pound's movement between the two buckets explicit — nothing is left to infer from the closing balances alone.
  • "Normal (No-Go)" marks years where the guardrail is structurally switched off (age 86+ by default), distinct from a genuinely unremarkable in-range "Normal" year.

Review Questions:

  1. (Practical) If the Classic Best and SG Worst cohorts happen to be the same start year, how many ledger sheets does the workbook create for that year?
  2. (Conceptual) Why does final value work as a tiebreaker for Classic's ranking specifically under "Always rises with inflation"?
  3. (Conceptual) Each ledger sheet lists a separate equity return column for Classic and for SG. Why might these show genuinely different values for the very same calendar year?
  4. (Practical) In a ledger sheet, a row shows a positive Cash Refill figure. What does that tell you about the SG Guardrail label on that same row?

MINI-CHALLENGE

Download the workbook for the default scenario. On the Summary sheet, find the row tagged "Worst" for Classic and confirm its Final Value is the lowest among all surviving Classic cohorts. Then open its matching ledger sheet and confirm the final year's SG Total matches the SG Final Value shown for that start year on the Summary sheet.

Appendix B — Glossary

  • Cohort: one complete, real, lived retirement starting in one specific historical year.
  • Total Lifetime Spending Delivered: the sum of everything a strategy actually paid out across a full cohort — this tool's primary headline metric, deliberately preferred over final pot value.
  • Final Value: the leftover pot at the end of the horizon — spare capacity, not a scoreboard.
  • Spare Capacity: the extra amount, found by genuine re-simulation, that could have been safely withdrawn in a given cohort without running out.
  • Hardship Year: a year in which actual spend fell below 80% of the strategy's target.
  • Guyton-Klinger guardrail: a rule cutting withdrawals 10% (or more, if graduated) when the withdrawal rate rises too far above target, permitting a 10% raise when it falls too far below a baseline.
  • Defensive Draw Threshold: the rule deciding whether a period's SG spending comes from Equities or Cash.
  • Ruin / Ran Out: the year, if any, a strategy's pot was fully exhausted within the horizon.
  • Rank tag (Best / Median / Worst): a cohort's standing for one strategy, by lifetime spend delivered, tie-broken by final value.
